# Where the campaign could go next — a proposal for new fronts

**Status: proposal. Nothing here has been filed as canon.** This document
exists so the options can be argued about before anyone writes a conflict
record. Pick one, pick three, or reject all of them — but the diagnosis in the
first section stands regardless.

---

## The diagnosis

The archive knows about **31 nations**. It has **9 written conflicts**.

Of those nine, here is where they actually happen:

| Conflict | Status | Whose soil |
|---|---|---|
| Mushroom Kingdom Civil War | active | Mushroom Kingdom |
| Raventree Manor Crisis | active | Mushroom-adjacent / planar |
| Vigilance Crew Incidents | active | the party's own ship |
| The Aegis Uprising | active | Regal Empire |
| The Dragon Conspiracy | active | cross-border, Empire-facing |
| The Primordial Wyrm Crisis | resolved | Koopa Dominion |
| Isle Delfino Conflict | active | Isle Delfino |
| Kong-Kremling Cold War | active | Kongo Bongo |
| The Koopa Resistance | integrated | Koopa Dominion |

Two of the nine are outside the Mushroom/Empire axis and **both are frozen**.
Kong-Kremling is explicitly a *cold* war — it has two filed battles in fifty-one.
Isle Delfino has eleven battles but they are all the same trial-and-riot
sequence.

So the working map is: **the Mushroom Kingdom is eating itself, the Regal Empire
is pressing on it, and everywhere else is scenery.** Twenty-nine nations have
census data, leaders, cultures and borders, and no reason for the party to ever
sail there.

That is not a lore problem. The lore is there — it is genuinely good, and that
is the frustrating part. It is a **pressure problem**. A nation only becomes a
front when it has something it is about to lose.

### What makes a good new front (the filter used below)

1. **It must already be in the archive.** No inventing a kingdom. Every option
   below is an existing `nations.json` record with existing text.
2. **It must generate a conflict the Mushroom Kingdom cannot absorb.** If the
   answer is "and then the Regency sends envoys", it is a subplot, not a front.
3. **It must give the party a reason to be there that isn't tourism.** Ideally
   a reason tied to something they already did.
4. **It must be able to go badly.** A front the party cannot lose is a set
   piece.

---

## Option A — Sarasaland: the stable neighbour is the target

**The strongest option, and the one with the least writing required.**

The archive describes Sarasaland as *"one of the few regional states that still
looks like it understands cause and effect"* — Daisy's government moves faster
than the Regency, hosts summits nobody else can host, and is called out as
**a stabilizing power**.

That is a description of a target.

Everyone in this setting is currently unstable. There is exactly one functioning
government in the Mushroom sphere, and it is small, independent, and next door to
an empire whose entire ideology is that chaos must be suppressed by force. The
Regal Empire does not need to invade Sarasaland. It needs to **offer to help**.

The front writes itself:

- Sarasaland's competence makes it the natural convening power for everyone
  fleeing the civil war. Refugees, exiled Loyalists, defecting Legion officers.
- That influx makes it a security problem, which makes it an Imperial interest.
- The Empire offers "stabilisation assistance". Accepting it ends Sarasaland's
  independence by administration rather than conquest — which is exactly the
  Iron Legion's established method (see the Iron Mandate).
- The dynasty archive already has **Lady Petal Rosewood in secret
  correspondence with Princess Daisy**, "planning something", gated at intel 45.
  That thread exists and has never paid off.

**Why it's good:** it is a war fought with treaties, not battles, which the
archive has never done. It reuses the Rosewood/Daisy hook. And the failure
state is quiet — the party can lose Sarasaland without a single battle record,
by simply not being there when the paperwork is signed.

**Cost to build:** one conflict record, three or four battles (mostly political
incidents), and promoting the existing Daisy–Rosewood correspondence to a lead.

## Option B — The Monster Underground: the treaty layers are already breached

The archive states this outright and then drops it: the Underground was a myth
*"until the Iron Legion's Gamma Division breached the treaty layers."*

**Gamma Division has already invaded a sovereign nation and there is no conflict
record for it.** That is a front sitting in the data waiting for someone to
notice. There is a whole faction (`undertale_monsters`), a king and queen, a
mountain, a treaty, a breach, and an aggressor — and zero filed battles.

- The Underground's defence is secrecy, and secrecy has already failed. What
  does a nation do when its only strategy is gone?
- 28 of the 34 characters linked to a dynasty house are in the **Snowdin Bone
  Line**. The largest single bloodline in the dynasty archive lives here. The
  house panel work just made that visible on every one of their pages, and
  they have no war to be part of.
- Gamma Division's own record describes it as a *"troubling symptom"* of
  Imperial policy. Giving it a victim makes it a villain instead of a memo.

**Why it's good:** it is the only option where a huge, already-written cast
becomes playable. It also gives the Regal Empire a crime that isn't
administrative — a straightforward invasion the party can be angry about.

**Cost:** one conflict record, and the Bone Line cast needs roles assigned.
Higher writing cost than A, much higher payoff in existing-content activation.

## Option C — Kongo Bongo: thaw the cold war

The cheapest possible front, because the conflict record **already exists** —
it is just frozen at two battles.

The archive's framing is the hook: Kongo Bongo matters *"especially whenever
King K. Rool remembers he wants to be a monarch again."* So have him remember.

- The trigger should come from the main plot rather than from K. Rool's mood.
  The obvious one: the Vigilance/Legionlance seizure changed who controls the
  southern shipping lanes. Banana economics is a supply chain, and someone just
  took the airship that protected it.
- This turns the DK Crew from "regional players who get dismissed" (their own
  faction text complains about exactly this) into a naval power with a grudge.

**Why it's good:** lowest cost of any option. It reuses an existing conflict
record, an existing faction rivalry, two existing banners, and it connects
directly to the Iron Sky Breach the party already lived through.

**Why it's weaker:** it doesn't broaden the campaign's *themes*, only its map.
It is another war about territory, and the archive has several.

## Option D — Equestria: the front that isn't a war

Included because it is the most distinctive thing in `nations.json` and nobody
has touched it.

The record is remarkable: a harmony-state that consolidated into a **pony-only
citizenship regime** after the Equality Pony takeover, where the old symbols
survived but "elasticity was replaced by administrative sameness". Changelings,
dragons, griffons and Saddle Arabians are reclassified as border problems.
Waluigi files it as *"a stained-glass state — beautiful from a distance, sharp
when cracked."*

This is a civil-rights front, not a military one. The conflict is over who
counts as a citizen, and it is won or lost in registries, language laws and
exemption paperwork — the same machinery the Iron Legion uses, running in a
country that thinks of itself as the good guys.

**Why it's good:** it is thematically unlike anything else on the board, and it
would let the investigation system do what it is actually best at. The
evidence-and-analysis format is far better suited to documenting an
administrative injustice than to narrating a battle. An exhibit that is a
*citizenship application form* is more frightening than one that is a sword.

**Why it's risky:** it needs to be written with real care, and it has no
existing battles, factions or characters to lean on. Highest writing cost here
by a wide margin.

---

## Recommendation

**Do A and B. Consider C as filler. Hold D until someone wants to write it
properly.**

A and B together fix the structural problem rather than just adding pins to the
map, because they pull in opposite directions:

- **Sarasaland (A)** is the Empire winning *without fighting* — a front where
  the threat is competence and paperwork, and the party's enemy is a signature.
- **The Underground (B)** is the Empire fighting *without pretext* — a front
  where the threat is a division that has already crossed a border, and the
  party's enemy is a soldier.

Run both and the Regal Empire stops being one antagonist with one method. It
becomes a state that annexes its friends and invades its neighbours in the same
fiscal year, which is a far better villain than the civil war has managed so
far. It also means the Mushroom Kingdom stops being the only place the story
can happen — because the party will be forced to choose which one they can get
to in time, and choosing means losing the other.

That is the actual fix. Not more nations. **A reason to be somewhere else, and
a cost for not being there.**

## If this is approved, the filing order

1. `conflicts.json` — two new records (`sarasaland_stabilisation`,
   `underground_breach`), following the existing shape.
2. `majorBattles.json` — each new conflict needs at least three battles or it
   will render as an empty front in the battle hub.
3. `factions.json` — Sarasaland's government needs a faction record; the
   Underground has one already.
4. Leads inside the relevant investigation files, so the party has a documented
   reason to care before the first battle is fought.
5. Banners for any new faction (see the faction banner sheet work).
